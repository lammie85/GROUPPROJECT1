VEHICLE & MOTORCYCLE MECHANICAL SUPPORT — KNOWLEDGE BASE
GENERAL MAINTENANCE, TROUBLESHOOTING & REPAIR GUIDANCE

PURPOSE
This document provides general answers to common mechanical questions about cars,
vans, trucks and motorcycles. It is intended for a mechanical-support assistant,
workshop help desk or troubleshooting knowledge base.

IMPORTANT SAFETY NOTICE
This guide provides general information, not a substitute for the vehicle
manufacturer's service manual or inspection by a qualified mechanic. Always use
the correct vehicle-specific specifications. Do not work under a vehicle supported
only by a jack. Disconnect the battery when a repair procedure requires it, taking
care with systems that require electronic resets. Never open a hot radiator or
coolant reservoir. If a vehicle has a fuel leak, smoke, burning smell, brake failure,
steering failure or a serious warning light, stop safely and seek professional help.
Never advise a user to drive a vehicle that may be unsafe.

ENGINE STARTING & BATTERY
Q: Why will my vehicle or motorcycle not start?
A: Common causes include a weak or discharged battery, loose or corroded battery
terminals, an empty fuel tank, a faulty starter motor, a failed starter relay, a
blown fuse, an ignition fault or an engine immobiliser issue. Check the fuel level
and whether the dashboard or lights come on. Listen for a click or slow cranking.
Do not repeatedly crank the engine for long periods. If basic checks do not identify
the problem, arrange a battery and starting-system test.

Q: What does a clicking sound when starting mean?
A: Rapid clicking often indicates low battery voltage or poor battery connections.
A single click may indicate a starter solenoid, starter motor or wiring fault.
Have the battery tested and terminals inspected before replacing components.

Q: How do I know if the battery is failing?
A: Possible signs include slow engine cranking, dim lights, dashboard resets,
difficulty starting and repeated need for jump-starting. A battery test is needed
to confirm its condition. Charging-system faults can also cause repeated battery
failure.

Q: Can I jump-start any vehicle or motorcycle?
A: No. Follow the manufacturer's jump-start procedure and confirm the system
voltage and battery type. Some motorcycles and modern vehicles have sensitive
electronics. Never jump-start a damaged, leaking, frozen or swollen battery.
If unsure, use roadside assistance or a qualified technician.

ENGINE OVERHEATING & COOLING SYSTEM
Q: Why is my engine overheating?
A: Possible causes include low coolant, coolant leaks, a failed radiator fan,
a faulty thermostat, a blocked radiator, a damaged water pump or a failed
radiator cap. Stop in a safe place and switch off the engine if the temperature
warning appears. Do not remove the radiator cap while hot. Allow the engine to
cool and arrange an inspection.

Q: What should I do if coolant is leaking?
A: Do not continue driving if the leak is significant or the temperature rises.
After the engine has cooled, check for visible leaks around hoses, radiator,
reservoir and water pump. Do not put your hands near moving fans or belts.
A mechanic should pressure-test the cooling system and repair the leak.

Q: Can I use water instead of coolant?
A: Water may be an emergency temporary measure only where the manufacturer's
instructions permit it. Use the specified coolant mixture as soon as possible.
Incorrect coolant or prolonged use of plain water can cause corrosion, deposits
or freezing-related damage.

ENGINE OIL & LUBRICATION
Q: How often should engine oil be changed?
A: Follow the manufacturer's service schedule, using the specified oil grade
and quality. The interval depends on the engine, oil type, mileage, operating
conditions and time. Severe traffic, dust, frequent short trips and heavy loads
may require more frequent servicing.

Q: What happens if engine oil is low?
A: Low oil can cause excessive friction, overheating and serious engine damage.
Check the oil level on level ground using the procedure in the owner's manual.
If the oil-pressure warning light comes on while the engine is running, stop
safely and switch off the engine. Do not continue driving until the cause is
checked.

Q: Why is my engine using too much oil?
A: Possible causes include external leaks, worn piston rings, valve-seal wear,
turbocharger problems or an incorrect oil grade. Record how much oil is added
and over what distance, inspect for leaks and have the engine assessed.

BRAKES & BRAKE SYSTEM
Q: Why are my brakes making a squealing or grinding noise?
A: Squealing may be caused by worn pads, dust, glazing or vibration. Grinding
can indicate severely worn pads or metal-to-metal contact. Grinding, reduced
braking performance or a brake warning light requires immediate inspection.
Do not continue driving if braking is unsafe.

Q: Why does the brake pedal feel soft or sink?
A: Possible causes include air in the hydraulic system, a fluid leak, worn
components or a failing master cylinder. Stop driving and arrange professional
inspection. Do not attempt to solve a soft brake pedal by simply topping up fluid.

Q: How often should brake pads be replaced?
A: There is no single interval for every vehicle. Replacement depends on pad
thickness, driving conditions, load and manufacturer limits. Have pads, discs,
calipers and brake fluid inspected at scheduled services.

TYRES, WHEELS & SUSPENSION
Q: Why does my vehicle vibrate while driving?
A: Common causes include unbalanced wheels, damaged tyres, incorrect tyre
pressure, bent wheels, worn suspension parts or drivetrain faults. If vibration
is severe, sudden or accompanied by unusual noise, stop safely and arrange
inspection.

Q: Why does my vehicle pull to one side?
A: Possible causes include unequal tyre pressures, wheel-alignment problems,
uneven tyre wear, a sticking brake or worn suspension components. Check tyre
pressures against the vehicle label or manual. Have the steering and brakes
inspected if the problem continues.

Q: What tyre pressure should I use?
A: Use the pressure specified on the vehicle's tyre-information label or in the
owner's manual. Do not rely solely on the maximum pressure printed on the tyre.
Check pressures when tyres are cold and use the correct specification for load.

Q: When should tyres be replaced?
A: Replace tyres when tread reaches the legal or manufacturer minimum, when
there is exposed cord, bulging, deep cuts, sidewall damage, persistent pressure
loss or age-related deterioration. A damaged sidewall should be assessed
immediately by a tyre professional.

TRANSMISSION, CLUTCH & DRIVE SYSTEM
Q: Why is my manual vehicle difficult to change gears?
A: Possible causes include low or incorrect transmission fluid, clutch adjustment
or wear, hydraulic clutch faults, worn linkage or internal gearbox damage.
Avoid forcing the gear lever. Arrange inspection if gears grind or engagement
is difficult.

Q: Why does my clutch slip?
A: A slipping clutch may cause engine speed to rise without a matching increase
in road speed. Common causes include worn friction material, oil contamination
or a pressure-plate fault. Have it inspected promptly; continued use can cause
further damage.

Q: Why does an automatic transmission jerk or delay shifting?
A: Possible causes include incorrect or degraded transmission fluid, electronic
control faults, sensor problems or internal wear. Check the owner's manual for
the correct fluid-check procedure. Do not add universal fluid or additives unless
the manufacturer specifies them.

MOTORCYCLE-SPECIFIC QUESTIONS
Q: Why does my motorcycle stall at idle?
A: Possible causes include incorrect idle speed, a dirty air filter, fuel-delivery
problems, a vacuum leak, spark-plug faults or sensor issues on fuel-injected
models. Check the service schedule and have the fuel and ignition systems
inspected.

Q: Why does my motorcycle chain make noise?
A: The chain may be dry, too loose, too tight, worn or incorrectly aligned.
Inspect and lubricate it according to the owner's manual. Measure chain slack
at the specified point and adjust to the manufacturer's specification. Replace
a chain and sprockets when wear limits are reached.

Q: Why does my motorcycle lose power?
A: Possible causes include restricted airflow, fuel-delivery problems, a blocked
exhaust, ignition faults, low compression or overheating. Avoid high-speed riding
until the cause is identified.

Q: Why does my motorcycle wobble?
A: Possible causes include incorrect tyre pressure, damaged tyres, loose wheel
bearings, steering-head bearing wear, wheel misalignment or suspension faults.
Reduce speed gradually, avoid abrupt steering or braking, stop safely and arrange
inspection. Do not continue riding if the wobble persists.

ELECTRICAL SYSTEM & WARNING LIGHTS
Q: Why does my battery keep going flat?
A: Possible causes include an ageing battery, faulty alternator or motorcycle
charging system, loose connections, parasitic electrical drain or accessories
left switched on. A technician should test battery condition, charging voltage
and key-off current draw.

Q: What should I do when a warning light appears?
A: Identify the symbol using the owner's manual. Red warnings, oil-pressure
warnings, overheating warnings and brake-system warnings generally require
stopping as soon as it is safe. An amber warning often indicates a fault that
needs diagnosis soon. Do not ignore warning lights or clear fault codes without
addressing the underlying issue.

Q: What does the check-engine light mean?
A: It indicates that the engine-management system has detected a fault. The
cause may be minor or serious. A diagnostic scan can retrieve fault codes, but
the code alone does not prove which component must be replaced. Diagnose the
system before replacing parts. If the light flashes, the engine runs roughly or
power drops significantly, stop safely and seek professional assistance.

FUEL, EXHAUST & PERFORMANCE
Q: Why is fuel consumption higher than normal?
A: Possible causes include low tyre pressure, a clogged air filter, faulty sensors,
poor-quality fuel, dragging brakes, incorrect engine temperature or changes in
driving conditions. Compare consumption over several refills and have the
engine and fuel systems checked if the change persists.

Q: Why is black, blue or white smoke coming from the exhaust?
A: Black smoke can indicate excess fuel or incomplete combustion. Blue smoke
often indicates engine oil burning. Persistent thick white smoke after warm-up
may indicate coolant entering the combustion chamber, although condensation
can produce light vapour when cold. Stop and seek inspection if smoke is heavy,
persistent or accompanied by overheating or fluid loss.

ROUTINE PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE
- Follow the vehicle manufacturer's service schedule and use approved parts
  and fluids of the specified grade.
- Check engine oil, coolant, brake fluid and other service fluids at the intervals
  stated in the owner's manual.
- Inspect tyres for pressure, tread wear, cuts and bulges.
- Check lights, indicators, horn, mirrors, wipers and washers.
- Inspect brake operation and listen for unusual noises.
- For motorcycles, inspect chain condition, lubrication, slack, sprockets,
  controls, lights and tyre condition.
- Replace filters, spark plugs, belts and other service items at the specified
  intervals.
- Keep records of mileage, service dates, repairs and parts replaced.

DIAGNOSTIC & REPAIR WORKFLOW
1. Collect the vehicle make, model, year, engine size and mileage.
2. Ask whether the vehicle is a car, van, truck or motorcycle.
3. Record the exact symptom, when it occurs and whether it is constant or
   intermittent.
4. Ask about warning lights, unusual sounds, smells, leaks, smoke or vibration.
5. Ask when the problem began and whether recent repairs, servicing, accidents
   or part replacements occurred.
6. Perform safe visual checks and use suitable diagnostic equipment.
7. Compare findings with manufacturer specifications and service information.
8. Identify the root cause before recommending parts or repairs.
9. Explain the repair, required parts, estimated labour and risks before work
   begins.
10. Test the repair safely and document the work completed.

INFORMATION TO COLLECT BEFORE ANSWERING A MECHANICAL QUESTION
- Vehicle type: car, van, truck or motorcycle
- Make and model
- Year of manufacture
- Engine size and fuel type
- Current mileage or odometer reading
- Exact problem or symptom
- When the problem occurs: starting, idle, acceleration, braking or cruising
- Warning lights or diagnostic trouble codes
- Recent maintenance or repairs
- Any visible leak, smoke, unusual sound, smell or vibration

ASSISTANT RESPONSE RULES
- Give clear, practical and cautious explanations in plain language.
- Ask for the vehicle make, model, year and symptoms when needed.
- Start with safe, simple checks; do not recommend risky roadside repairs.
- Never guess a torque value, fluid capacity, tyre pressure, wiring connection,
  service interval or part specification. Ask for vehicle details and consult
  manufacturer information.
- A diagnostic trouble code is evidence of a detected system fault, not automatic
  proof that a named part has failed.
- Do not recommend replacing expensive components without testing.
- Explain when a qualified mechanic, auto electrician or specialist is required.
- Do not instruct users to bypass safety systems, airbags, ABS, immobilisers,
  emissions controls or other protective equipment.
- Do not advise driving or riding when brakes, steering, tyres, wheels, fuel
  systems or engine temperature may make the vehicle unsafe.
- For severe or uncertain faults, recommend stopping safely and arranging
  professional inspection.
